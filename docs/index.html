How a pick is made
  1. Every finished box score is stored. Each player's production per minute is weighted toward recent games; last season counts at half weight.
  2. Minutes are projected from the last ~5 games (blended with last season early on), and trimmed on back-to-backs.
  3. The opponent's tendency to allow each stat adjusts the projection, shrunk toward average until it has enough games.
  4. A player-specific distribution turns the projection into a probability for any line.
  5. Safe picks need ≥85% from the model and ≥85% hit rate over the last 20 games. Market picks must beat the sportsbooks' no-vig price by 5+ points and agree with recent form.
  6. Matchup checks. Every stat has its own: rebounds look at the opponent's rebounding share, rebound chances, size and position defense; free throws at the FT rate they allow, their fouling, position defense and home/away; points at shooting allowed, rim protection and the Vegas team total; and so on. Each check is weighted by how much it actually predicted results in past games. Teammates and opponents who are out are part of it: their share of shots, assists, rebounds and minutes is redistributed. The line is set from the player's own numbers, and the matchup must then favor it by at least +1%. Last season this hit 89-90%, vs 86.5-87% for the original engine.
  7. Players who are injured, listed questionable, low-minute or volatile are excluded. Lineups take one leg per team, at most two per game, and prefer the strongest matchups.
